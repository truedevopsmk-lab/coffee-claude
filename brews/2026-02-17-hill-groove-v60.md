## ⏱ Brew Steps (4:6 Method)

1. Rinse filter thoroughly and preheat server  
2. Add **15 g** freshly ground coffee  
3. Start timer and pour **40 ml** (first 40%)  
4. At **0:45**, pour next **40 ml**  
5. Complete first phase by **~1:15**  
6. Pour remaining **60%** in 3 equal pours (~40 ml each)  
7. Maintain gentle spiral pours  
8. Finish pouring by **~2:15**  
9. Allow drawdown to complete around **2:45–3:00**

---

## 👅 Cup Profile & Notes

- **Aroma:** Vibrant florals, sweet dried fruit  
- **Taste:** Floral dominance while hot, evolving into plum sweetness and wine-like tanginess  
- **Acidity:** Present but controlled  
- **Body:** Light–medium, tea-like  
- **Finish:** Clean, oolong-tea-like with lingering sweetness  

A layered and evolving cup. The acidity feels refined rather than sharp, and sweetness becomes more expressive as the temperature drops.

---

## 🔧 Adjustment Notes

- Increase first pour slightly for brighter acidity  
- Lower temperature to **90–91°C** if acidity feels sharp  
- Grind 1 click finer for more body  
- Grind 1 click coarser for higher clarity  

---

## 📝 Journal Thoughts

The dry aroma was immediately vibrant and floral — energizing even before brewing.

Using the 4:6 method structured the cup beautifully. Florals dominated early while hot, elegant and expressive. As it cooled — my favorite phase — the plum sweetness deepened and the body turned softly tea-like.

Further down the temperature curve, the wine-like character emerged with nuance and gentle tanginess. The 4:6 method mellowed the acidity nicely, keeping it refined rather than aggressive.

North East Indian coffees continue to surprise me — expressive, unique, and full of character.
